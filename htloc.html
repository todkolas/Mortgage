Part 1

What it is

The HELOC strategy stripped down to its core mechanic.

The plain HELOC strategy (often called HELOC chunking) drops the paycheck-parking trick from velocity banking and keeps just one move: borrow a lump sum from your HELOC, dump it on your mortgage principal, and pay the HELOC down from your monthly cash flow surplus.

Once the HELOC is paid back to zero, you take another lump sum and repeat. The cycle continues until the mortgage is gone.

"I'll borrow $20,000 from my HELOC, smash it onto my mortgage principal, then aggressively pay back the HELOC. Repeat every 14 months. My 25-year mortgage is gone in 8."

It feels powerful. It looks aggressive. The math says otherwise.

Part 2

How it works

A concrete example with round numbers.

Setup
  • Mortgage: $400,000 at 5%, 25-year amortization
  • Monthly mortgage payment: ~$2,330
  • HELOC limit: $50,000 at 6%
  • Monthly cash flow surplus: $1,500
Step 1

Take a chunk from HELOC, smash the mortgage

Borrow $20,000 from HELOC. Apply directly to mortgage principal as a lump-sum prepayment.

Mortgage drops from $400,000 to $380,000. HELOC balance is now $20,000.

Step 2

Continue mortgage payments + aggressively pay HELOC

Each month: make your regular $2,330 mortgage payment AND send your full $1,500 surplus to the HELOC.

HELOC interest accumulates daily — but the balance shrinks every month.

Step 3

HELOC paid off, repeat

After roughly 14 months, the HELOC balance is back to $0. Total HELOC interest paid: about $700.

Now take another $20,000 chunk, smash the mortgage again, restart the cycle.

Step 4

Continue until mortgage is gone

You'll do this cycle ~15–18 times over 12–14 years instead of paying for the full 25.

That's the entire strategy. No paycheck juggling, no using the HELOC as a chequing account. Just chunks in, surplus out.

Part 3

Why people think it works

The intuition is compelling — and incomplete.

The argument from the YouTube videos and forum posts goes like this:

"Mortgage interest is calculated on the entire balance, every single month, for 25 to 30 years. By killing $20,000 of principal right now, I just saved myself decades of compounding interest. The HELOC interest I pay over 14 months is nothing compared to that."

The first half of that sentence is true. The second half is true on its own. But put together, they conceal a subtle error.

The hidden assumption

The argument assumes the only alternative to chunking is doing nothing — paying the mortgage on its normal 25-year schedule. Compared to that baseline, chunking does save a lot of interest.

But that's the wrong baseline. The real comparison is:

  • Strategy A — HELOC chunking: Send $1,500/month surplus to the HELOC after taking a chunk
  • Strategy B — Direct extra payments: Send $1,500/month surplus directly to the mortgage

Both strategies use the same amount of cash flow. Both end up paying off the mortgage years faster than the 25-year baseline. The question is: which one does it more efficiently?

Part 4

The actual math

A side-by-side walk-through using the same example.

Both scenarios start with the same $400,000 mortgage at 5% and the same $1,500/month surplus. Let's compare what happens after the first 14 months.

Scenario A

HELOC Chunking

  • Month 0: Take $20K from HELOC, apply to mortgage. Mortgage: $380K. HELOC: $20K.
  • Months 1–14: Normal mortgage payment + $1,500/month to HELOC.
  • Month 14: HELOC balance = $0. Mortgage ≈ $369,400.
  • HELOC interest paid: ~$700
  • Mortgage interest paid: ~$21,900
  • Total interest paid: ~$22,600
Scenario B

Direct Extra Payments

  • Month 0: No HELOC. Just normal mortgage.
  • Months 1–14: Normal mortgage payment + $1,500/month directly to mortgage principal.
  • Month 14: Mortgage ≈ $368,400.
  • HELOC interest paid: $0
  • Mortgage interest paid: ~$22,400
  • Total interest paid: ~$22,400

What this shows

After 14 months, both scenarios end up at almost the same place:

  • Roughly identical mortgage balance (B is about $1,000 lower)
  • Roughly identical total interest paid (B is about $200 lower)
  • Same total cash spent

The difference is small but consistently in B's favor — by approximately the amount of HELOC interest you paid in A. Run this for the full 12–14 years it takes to pay off the mortgage and the gap compounds to $3,000–$8,000 saved by direct extra payments versus chunking.

Why this happens

In Scenario A, your $20K lump sum reduces mortgage principal at month 0. That earns you interest savings over the 14-month cycle. Good.

But your $1,500/month surplus is going to the HELOC, not the mortgage — so the mortgage is amortizing slowly during those 14 months.

In Scenario B, your $1,500/month goes straight to the mortgage. There's no big upfront drop, but each month's $1,500 starts saving interest immediately.

By month 14, the cumulative principal reduction in B (~$21K) actually slightly exceeds A's $20K lump. And B never paid HELOC interest. B wins by a small but real margin every single time HELOC rate ≥ mortgage rate.

The general rule

The cost of HELOC chunking is approximately:

Total HELOC interest paid over the life of the strategy

If you did 15 chunks of $20K each, paying ~$700 in HELOC interest per cycle, that's ~$10,500 in extra interest paid for nothing compared to direct extra payments.

This flips only if HELOC rate is meaningfully lower than mortgage rate — rare in Canada, since HELOCs are usually Prime + 0.5%–1% and mortgages are typically near or below Prime.

Part 5

When it makes sense

Three legitimate cases where chunking beats direct payments.

1. Your mortgage caps prepayments

Many closed Canadian mortgages limit annual prepayment to 10–20% of the original balance. If you have surplus cash flow that exceeds that cap, chunking lets you put it to work anyway.

Example

Original mortgage: $500,000. Prepayment cap: 15% per year = $75,000.
You have $100,000 in surplus this year. The mortgage will only let you apply $75,000.
Chunking: take $25,000 from HELOC, pay to mortgage immediately under the cap, then pay HELOC down with future surplus. Wait for next year, repeat.

2. HELOC rate is below mortgage rate

If your HELOC is genuinely cheaper than your mortgage (rare, but possible if you have a high-rate mortgage and a great HELOC), every dollar moved temporarily to the HELOC saves you the rate difference.

Verify by checking both rates. If HELOC ≥ mortgage, this case doesn't apply.

3. You need the discipline structure

Some people pay down a HELOC balance more aggressively than they would make extra mortgage payments. The visible "debt to clear" creates urgency.

This is a real psychological effect. But it costs you the HELOC interest as a "discipline tax." A free alternative: set up an automatic biweekly accelerated mortgage payment plus a fixed monthly prepayment — same forced behaviour, zero HELOC fees.

Part 6

When to skip it

The default answer for most Canadian mortgages.

  • Your mortgage allows unlimited prepayment — most variable-rate mortgages and many fixed-rate ones do. Just make extra payments directly.
  • HELOC rate is higher than mortgage rate — the rate spread eats your savings.
  • You don't have stable surplus cash flow — chunking works only if you can reliably pay down the HELOC each month. Variable income makes this dangerous.
  • You already make extra mortgage payments — adding the HELOC step just creates extra interest cost without changing anything else.
  • You don't have a healthy emergency fund — taking $20K from your HELOC reduces your available emergency credit. If something goes wrong, you've lost your buffer.

Verdict

The honest bottom line

Skip in most cases

If your mortgage allows prepayments, just send your surplus directly to the mortgage. Same outcome, no HELOC interest paid, less complexity, and your HELOC stays available as an emergency credit line.

Use only if

Your closed mortgage has a hard prepayment cap that you're hitting every year, AND you have surplus cash flow above that cap. In that narrow case, chunking lets you put the extra money to work — at the cost of HELOC interest.
